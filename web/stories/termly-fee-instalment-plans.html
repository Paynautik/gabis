A termly fee plan that parents can actually keep

Asking a family to find a whole term of fees on day one is how balances end up overdue. A plan they can meet, with reminders that arrive in time, is how they get paid.

The gabis team · October 2, 2026

Why lump-sum fees fall behind

Most parents are not refusing to pay. A full term of fees arrives as one large number, and when it does not fit into a single month, the invoice simply sits. The bursar then spends the term reconciling by hand and chasing individual families, and the school finds out it is short only after the term has ended.

Build the plan before the term starts

  • Split across the term. Break the fee into a small number of dated instalments rather than one deadline.
  • Put every date in writing. Each parent should see their own balance and their own schedule, not a general notice.
  • Accept part payments. If a parent can pay part of an instalment now, let it count against the plan instead of marking the whole thing late.

Remind early, not only when late

A reminder that arrives ten days before an instalment is due lands while there is still time to plan for it. A reminder that arrives after it is missed is a different conversation. Reminding before each date, then again on the day, keeps most balances from ever becoming overdue.

Make paying effortless

Parents should not have to install anything or log in to settle a fee. A payment link sent over WhatsApp or email, which opens straight onto the amount owing, removes the friction that quietly turns into late payments.

Know where you stand, by class

The bursar's real question is not "has this parent paid?" but "how much have we collected, and how much is outstanding, in each class?" A view of collected, outstanding and overdue balances by class shows where to focus before the term is over, instead of afterwards.

Follow up politely when a balance slips

When an instalment is missed, a courteous call or message in the parent's own language, asking for a payment date, resolves far more than a stern letter. It also keeps the relationship intact, which matters for a school that expects the family to stay for years.

How gabis handles this

Issue a whole term at once: one fee run reaches every parent on the roll, each with their own balance and schedule. Instalment plans are the default for tuition, and part payments track against the plan. Reminders arrive ten days before each date. Parents pay from a link on WhatsApp or email and do not need the app. You see collected, outstanding and overdue by class, and overdue balances get a call in the parent's own language.
